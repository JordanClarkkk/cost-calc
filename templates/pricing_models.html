Pricing Model Scenarios

Five models under consideration as we move from a fixed-fee structure to a hybrid approach. No decision has been made.

These are reference points for understanding tradeoffs — the final model will likely be a hybrid.
1
Connection-Based Flat Tiers
Fixed monthly fee based on mapping bundles
Current Beta

Customers pay a fixed monthly fee determined by the number of active mappings, sold in bundles. An optional soft cap via credits could push this closer to Models 3 or 5.

Pros
  • Simple to sell and budget for
  • Predictable revenue
  • Low friction onboarding
Cons
  • Ignores actual cost to serve
  • Breaks for distributors & heavy users
  • Margin risk at high volume
2
Tiered Pricing with Feature Gating
Classic SaaS tiers — Basic / Advanced / Enterprise

Larger jumps between tiers unlock more connections and features. Encourages upsell but introduces cliff pricing: moving from 10 to 11 connections could mean a significant price increase.

Pros
  • Familiar SaaS sales motion
  • Predictable recurring revenue
  • Feature differentiation enables upsell
Cons
  • Cliff pricing triggers gaming & discounts
  • Doesn't defend margins without usage guardrails
  • Customers feel punished at tier edges
3
High Fixed Base + Low Metered Usage
Generous baseline included; cheap overages
AI SaaS Norm

A higher base fee covers platform access and a generous processing baseline. Additional usage beyond that baseline is metered at a low per-unit rate. Most cost is predictable for the client; overages are rarely a surprise.

Pros
  • Predictable for the customer
  • Strong margin protection at baseline
  • Becoming the norm in AI-powered SaaS
Cons
  • Hard sell for light or seasonal users
  • Customers feel they overpay in quiet months
  • High base can block SMB entry
4
Pure Pay-As-You-Go
No subscription — pay per processing run

No subscription fee. Customers pay per processing run, priced by volume and complexity. Standard for API and developer tools, but unusual in this market segment.

Pros
  • Perfect cost alignment for the company
  • Safest for margins at any volume
  • Zero commitment lowers barrier to try
Cons
  • Unpredictable bills worry CFOs
  • Zero revenue during idle months
  • Hardest to sell in this market segment
5
Low Fixed Base + High Metered Usage
Cheap entry; usage-heavy revenue model

A low base fee gives cheap platform access and a small included baseline. Usage beyond that is metered at a higher per-unit rate. Revenue depends heavily on actual usage — thin in quiet months, strong when usage is high.

Pros
  • Low entry barrier, easy to sell
  • Incentivises driving usage growth
  • Revenue scales with customer success
Cons
  • Risk of surprise charges if not communicated
  • Thin margins in low-usage months
  • Customers may self-limit to avoid overages
